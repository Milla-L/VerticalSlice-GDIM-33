## Milestone 3
1. My shader graph works as a post processing effect which causes an overlay of two textures. There is the base texture which draws out my basic flat image of the eye outlines. It goes in and out of transparency using a sine time graph that is shifted over by one and divided by 4 so that the texture does not completely blank out the screen and gives off a more diluted effect. The top overlay image is of the pupils of the eyes which offset by a vector2 of cosine and sine values multiplied by the normal time. From there it is fed into an offset before going into the sample texture's UV slot. After that, the same method that the base texture uses to go in and out of transparency is employed. After, the both texture processes go into another LERP to actually overlay them and feed into the Base Color of the Fragment to show on screen.

## Final Submission
1. My core gameplay loop is talking with the disembodied voice in a NPC to player dialogue loop. Each player's response goes into a new NPC dialogue line which improves or decreases the friendship bar on the left side of the screen. The back and forth dialogue results in one of four endings with three branches having their own unique endings and the rest having the default ending. This is exactly what I outlined for my basic gameplay loop. The only major difference is that in my game the player is stationary until the alternate endings rather than the original plan where the player was meant to move after every dialogue option. The gameplay does the true full gameplay accurately. If I were to flesh out a whole game, I would make it like this where there are several dialogue trees with the NPC and rather than moving at every moment, there would be a scene then movement and repeat. The full game would flesh out the rest of the story and have more chances to increase/ decrease the friendship bar to give more or less extreme consequences over just the new scenes that the friendship bar is used for now. 

2. The gameplay logic that my rendering effect is activated by is that if the friendship variable is below or equal to 50, then the rendering effect activates. There is a script graph named StandardEnd which holds the logic that handles a Boolean to declare the if statement. From there, in the Render Graph there is a branch which makes it so when the StandardEnd script is true, the friendship is greater than fifty, then the rendering effect plays out and is visible. If the StandardEnd script is false, then the render is changed to a transparent version that does not appear as anything.

3. The process in which I break down large projects into small parts has me listing out the high level of what I need to do and splitting it into smaller, more detailed subsections. I determine exactly what I need for everything to work, what I want to happen, and possibly how I want it to happen. Below that, I break up the steps into smaller portions that are actually viable. For example, I can say I want the player to click on a button to progress the story. From there I break up that step into make a button, make the button script, and hook the script up to the button. This makes it significantly easier to do exactly what I need to do when it needs to be done because it creates an order of events to follow as well as a baseline instruction sheet for how to complete the assignment in terms of several smaller assignments. In addition, I take the overall assignment and put it into miniature segments. I take the mechanics, aesthetics, and major features of the project as their own smaller projects to make the tasks more manageable and easier to schedule. By creating a list of todos, I can schedule around what needs to happen and allot time to each task in a much less crowded fashion than if I take the project as a massive block all at once. Rather than getting it done in one session, I can say I will do a task or two a day depending on how many smaller tasks that I have segmented.
